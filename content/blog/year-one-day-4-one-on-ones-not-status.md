# Day 4: A 1:1 Shouldn't Be a Status Update

I have a team of seven. Doing weekly 1:1s with everyone would take a significant amount of my time, so I decided to do them monthly instead. I usually keep the conversation short to 30–40 minutes and, whenever possible, do it face-to-face in the office, to keep these conversations as rich and honest as possible.

Early on, I noticed something: Some 1:1s started to feel like a monthly status report.

"I finished this. I worked on that. This project is delayed."

That's useful information—but I don't think a 1:1 is the best place for it.

Then I have the following fixed agenda plus any open topics brought from both sides:

- How are you really doing? Is everything fine?
- What's the achievement?
- Where are you getting stuck?
- What do you need from me?
- What are goals for the next 1:1 meeting?

If it's effective, 1:1s are a space for **managing up**. It's also a chance for team members to give me feedback, challenge my decisions, tell me what isn't working, and help me understand what I might be missing.

How do you use 1:1s to achieve with your manager or your team member?
